Part B: Interpretation

  1. The coefficient for budget, as a description of these movies is 0.77898. This means that given that a movie does not pass the Bechdel test, for every 1% increase in movie budget, domestic gross revenue is expected to increase 0.778%. The rate of change is in percentage because the model was created with log-transformed data.
  2. The coeffcient for the interaction term is 0.05204. This means that the rate of change in domestic gross revenue as it is related to budget is 0.05% higher for movies that pass the Bechdel test than those that fail the test.
  3. The F-statistic generated from the model is associated with a pvalue of 2.2e-16, suggesting that at least one factor built into this model is producing a significant effect on the response variable, domestic gross revenue. From the model output we can see that this factor is likely budget, which has a larger standardized t value and a significant p value, while the interaction term and coefficient for binaryPASS alone are not significant in this model. Therefore I conclude that budget probably does have some kind of effect on domestic gross revenue (which lines up with what I generally know about the movie industry), but that whether a movie passes or fails the Bechdel test likely does not have a significant effect on domestic gross revenue.

Part C: Diagnostic Plots

Normality: The Q-Q plot shows us that a majority of the points lie on the line, which indicates normality. At the tails, the points do start to fall off the line, but because the majority in the center of the distribution lay flat on the line, there can be a reasonable assumption that the data has normality.

Constant Variance: Looking at the Residuals vs. Fitted plot, the residuals seem to be generally randomly scattered around the 0 line, suggesting there is reason to assume the data has constant variance.

I think we can conclude that the model assumptions are reasonably met for this comparison. I would definitely still remain pretty skeptical of its overall fit and accuracy because there are a lot of extreme values. I also probably wouldn’t use this model to predict gross revenue for new movies, but I think it can be helpful to describe the general trend/relationship between movie budget and revenue.

Part B: Interpretation

For every $1 million increase in budget, the odds of a movie passing the Bechdel test decreases by 0.66%, which is a pretty negligible change and suggests budget does not have a significant effect on changing the odds of a movie passing or failing the Bechdel test. For every decade that passes, the odds of a movie passing the Bechdel test increases by 19.56%, which might suggest a significant effect. Lastly, for every 1-point increase in IMDB rating, the odds of a movie passing the Bechdel test decreases by 25.61%, which suggests an even stronger effect than release decade. According to this logistic regression, IMDB rating seems to have the strongest effect on the odds of a movie passing or failing the Bechdel test.
